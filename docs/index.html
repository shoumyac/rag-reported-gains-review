Do reported gains in retrieval-augmented generation survive scrutiny?

A pre-registered systematic review, meta-analysis and evaluation-validity audit. Anmita Das and Shoumya Chowdhury.

Status: protocol registered on 1 November 2026. The search has not been run and there are no results yet. Registration: doi.org/10.17605/OSF.IO/XGPCZ.

The question

Many papers on retrieval-augmented generation (RAG) report that a new method beats a baseline. We pool those improvements within each paper, ask which design and evaluation choices explain how much they vary, check how completely studies report what is needed to reproduce them, and re-run a pre-specified sample of methods to see whether their conclusions change under different metrics and LLM judges.

We never rank methods across papers, because their retrievers, generators and corpora differ.

Research questions

  1. What is the pooled reported improvement over each study's own baseline, and how much does it vary?
  2. How much of that variation do design and evaluation choices explain?
  3. How many studies report what is needed to reproduce and compare their results, and do less transparent or smaller studies report larger gains?
  4. What share of studies validated their automated metrics or LLM judges against human judgement?
  5. For a sample of re-run methods, do reported gains replicate, and do conclusions flip under other metrics and judges?

How we keep it honest

  • The protocol is frozen on OSF; every change is logged.
  • Citations are resolved against Crossref or DataCite by an automatic check.
  • Every number in the paper is produced by script from the data.
  • Every extracted value carries its page, table and quoted text.
  • Papers by the authors are flagged, checked by an independent person, and tested in a sensitivity analysis.
